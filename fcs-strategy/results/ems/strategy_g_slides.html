System Overview & Motivation

Why we need an EMS

  • Fuel cell has slow dynamics — ramp rate limited to 100 W/s
  • Motor load swings from 0 → 600+ W in milliseconds during pulse & acceleration
  • Supercapacitor bridges the transient gap — absorbs FC surplus, supplies burst demand
  • EMS must keep SC charge-neutral lap-to-lap while maximising FC efficiency

The Three Correction Terms

1. LPF Feedforward

P_filt += (1-α)·(P_motor − P_filt) α = e^(-0.2/15) ≈ 0.9868 τ = 15 s
  • Purpose: Smoothly follows average motor demand
  • Effect: Anchors FC near mean demand without reacting to every pulse
  • Key insight: Without this, PI alone overshoots on every pulse and the integrator saturates

τ=15s >> lap pulse time (~70s) → P_filt ≈ mean(P_motor) over several cycles

2. Kp Proportional

P_p = K_p · (SOC₀ − SOC) K_p = 2075 W/SOC SOC₀ = 0.60 (reference)
  • Purpose: Immediate correction when SC drifts from reference
  • Effect: Pulls FC output down when SOC > 0.60 (SC overcharged)
  • Effect: Pushes FC output up when SOC < 0.60 (SC depleted)

K_p tuned via bisection: [100 → 8000] range, converges in 2 iterations

3. Ki Integral

P_i = K_i · ∫(SOC₀−SOC) dt K_i = 2 W/(SOC·s) clamp: ±400/K_i = ±200 s·SOC
  • Purpose: Eliminates steady-state error; compensates lap-to-lap drift
  • Updates every timestep — including glide/coast
  • Builds −364W correction over race to offset 150W×170s glide energy

Widened clamp (±200 vs old ±75) is the key to charge-sustaining convergence

Combined command

P_cmd = P_filt + K_p·(SOC₀−SOC) + K_i·∫(SOC₀−SOC)dt + P_lap_offset

Final output

P_fc = clip(P_cmd, 150 W, 600 W)

Energy Balance Insight

Higher floor → more glide energy pre-loaded → lower pulse demand → FC operates at lower, more efficient power during pulse phase. The SC becomes an energy buffer that shifts FC operating point toward peak efficiency.

Additional 150W Benefits

  • FC membrane stays at operating temperature — no 30–60 s cold-start delay
  • Avoids thermal stress from repeated cold-start cycles
  • Integral accumulates charge-balancing correction during glide

Implementation & Simulink Mapping

Python control function signature

strategy_g_pi.py — make_strategy()
def strategy_fn(P_dem, SOC, P_fc_prev,
                t_in_lap, lap_idx):
    # 1. LPF feedforward
    P_filt = α*P_filt + (1-α)*P_dem

    # 2. SOC-PI controller
    soc_err = 0.60 - SOC
    integrator += soc_err * 0.2        # DT=0.2s
    integrator = clip(integrator,
                      -200, +200)      # anti-windup
    P_pi = 2075*soc_err + 2*integrator

    # 3. Lap supervisor (boundary detection)
    P_lap_offset += 0.3 * E_deficit/T_lap

    # 4. Combined + floor clip
    P_cmd = P_filt + P_pi + P_lap_offset
    return clip(P_cmd, 150, 600)       # [W]

Key Design Decisions & Lessons Learned

Why LPF + PI, not just PI?
Pure SOC-PI on a large supercapacitor (91 Wh) has slow response — the integrator saturates before SOC recovers. LPF feedforward anchors the FC near mean demand, so the PI only corrects small deviations. Together they converge; separately neither does.
Why 150 W floor (not lower)?
FC warm-up time: 30–60 s. Cold starts cause thermal stress and reduce membrane lifetime. At 150 W, energy pre-loading during glide is large enough (~25.5 kJ/cycle) to shift the pulse FC setpoint into the efficient 200–300 W zone, improving overall η.
Why does integral update during glide/coast?
If integral only accumulates during motor-on phases (~70 s/lap), it cannot build the −364 W correction needed to balance the 150 W × 170 s glide charging. Running the integrator through the full 237 s lap is essential for charge sustenance.
How was K_p tuned? (bisection)
Bisect K_p in [0, 3000]: run full 11-lap simulation, check ΔSOC. K_p=800 → ΔSOC=+0.018; K_p=2075 → ΔSOC=+0.007 ✓ Converges in 2 iterations due to widened clamp (±200 s·SOC).
SC ESR voltage sag — does it matter?
ESR = 80 mΩ. At peak motor current (I ≈ 4.6 A): 0.080 × 4.6 = 0.37 V sag on a ~50 V bus = 0.7%. Modelled in simulation. Confirmed negligible — SOC calculation error < 0.4%.
Why adaptive? (no drive-cycle dependency)
Strategies A–H are calibrated offline for the Silesia Ring profile. Strategy G uses only real-time measurements. On race day, if the team adopts a different speed profile or weather changes drag, Strategy G self-adapts — others would need re-tuning.
