The key insight: the motor is iron-loss-dominated

The BAFANG hub motor is inefficient at low steady power (~30–35%) because fixed iron losses dominate, but efficient under load (~55–60%).

~34%motor η @ cruise
~59%motor η @ pulse
165.3P&G km/m³

Pulse-and-Glide runs the motor hard in its efficient band, then switches it off to coast — avoiding the lossy low-power region entirely.

In our model P&G (165.3) beats a constant-velocity cruise by a wide margin — the profile, not the controller, is the first big lever.
Before the controller, we choose how to drive. The single most important fact about this powertrain: the hub motor wastes a fixed chunk of power to iron losses, so at a gentle cruise it only runs about a third efficient. Pulse-and-glide deliberately accelerates hard — where the motor is 55 to 60 percent efficient — then cuts the motor and coasts. That is why, in our model, pulse-and-glide beats a smooth constant-speed cruise by a wide margin.

Strategy G · Built From the Ground Up — 1/3

Three inputs the car already measures

The FC is slow (ramp ≤ 100 W/s); the motor swings 0 → ~1400 W in milliseconds. The supercap bridges the gap — and the controller decides how hard the FC works using only:

INPUT 1 — I_mot [A]

Motor (bus) current — Hall-effect sensor on the DC bus.

INPUT 2 — U_mot [V]

Motor bus voltage — measured at the controller input.

INPUT 3 — U_soc [V]

Supercap terminal voltage — voltage divider on the SC bank.

Derived: instantaneous motor power

P_motor = I_mot × U_mot

Derived: state of charge (no SOC sensor needed)

SOC = (U_soc² − V_min²) / (V_max² − V_min²) V_min = 40.0 V V_max = 60.8 V SOC₀ = 0.60
Exactly the three signals wired into the Strategy block of the main Simulink model — no maps, no GPS, no cycle knowledge.
Now we build the controller. The fuel cell is slow and the motor demand is spiky, so a supercapacitor sits in between. The controller's whole job is to set the fuel-cell power. It needs only three measurements the car already has: motor current, motor voltage, and supercap voltage. From the first two we get instantaneous motor power; from the third we get state of charge exactly, because a capacitor's energy is one-half C V squared. These three are precisely the inputs to the Strategy block in our Simulink model.

Combined command

P_cmd = LPF(P_motor) + K_p·(SOC₀−SOC) + K_i·∫(SOC₀−SOC)dt + P_lap_offset
P_fc = clip(P_cmd, 100 W, 1013 W)

Why each term earns its place

  • LPF alone can't correct SOC drift.
  • PI alone overshoots every pulse & the integrator saturates.
  • Together: LPF anchors, PI trims small errors → converges & charge-sustains.
Optional η-band cap (≈388 W) was tested but left off: on this high-drag car it just shoves peak energy through the SC (−3%) for no net gain.
Strategy G is not one idea, it's five, layered. Start with a low-pass filter of motor power: the fuel cell follows the slow average and ignores the spikes. That alone can't hold charge, so we add a proportional term on SOC error — push the cell harder when the cap drains. Add an integral term to kill slow drift, with an anti-windup clamp so it can't run away. Add a gentle per-lap offset. Finally, a hard floor of 100 watts keeps the membrane warm and pre-charges the cap. Each term fixes a specific failure of the one before. We also tried capping the cell at its efficiency sweet spot, but on this draggy car it just cycles energy through the cap and loses 3 percent, so it's off.

On the same velocity profile, all the legitimate strategies land within about two km per cubic metre of each other. Strategy G wins among the charge-sustaining ones at 165.3. Watch the two that look better — PI-only and the lookup table — they're cheating: their SOC ends far below where it started, so they're really just spending stored energy. Implementation is deliberately boring, which is good. The three inputs — motor current, motor voltage, supercap voltage — are exactly what already feeds the Strategy block in our main Simulink model, so this drops in without rewiring. And every term maps to a standard block: the low-pass is a discrete transfer function, the proportional and integral are a gain and a PID block with saturation for anti-windup, the lap offset is a triggered subsystem, and the floor-and-clip is a saturation block. No lookup tables at runtime — it's pure arithmetic on three live signals.

Honest caveats & next steps

  • ① Bench-validate the BAFANG efficiency map at cruise & pulse points.
  • ② Model regenerative braking — currently every stop's energy is discarded.
  • ③ Re-confirm losses vs last year's 280 km/m³ claim (converter, BOP, H₂ basis).
Bottom line: the controller is solved and near-optimal for this duty cycle — the next big gains are in the powertrain efficiency, not the EMS.
To wrap up: Strategy G gives 165.3 km per cubic metre, it's charge-neutral, and it needs only three signals the car already measures, so it's ready for Simulink and the ECU. But be honest about the big picture: the controller is basically solved — every sensible strategy is within two percent. The real lever is the motor efficiency map, which moves the answer by 25 percent. So the priorities are: validate the motor on the bench, add regenerative braking, and reconcile our honest loss model against last year's optimistic 280 number. The next gains come from the powertrain, not the energy manager.
